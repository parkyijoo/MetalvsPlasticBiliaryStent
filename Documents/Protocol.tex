\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

\textbf{Background and Significance}: Pancreatic cancer-related biliary obstruction is managed with biliary stent placement via ERCP. Current guidelines recommend metal biliary stents (MS) over plastic biliary stents (PS) for inoperable malignant obstruction; however, real-world comparative evidence on safety and effectiveness remains limited.

\textbf{Study Aims}: To compare the clinical safety and effectiveness of metal versus plastic biliary stents in patients with pancreatic cancer using real-world observational data from the OMOP Common Data Model.

\textbf{Study Description}:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Population}: Adult patients (age ≥ 18) with a prior diagnosis of pancreatic cancer who received their first biliary stent insertion, excluding those with a history of cholangiocarcinoma.
\item
  \textbf{Comparators}: Target --- Metal Biliary Stent (ATLAS Cohort ID: 688); Comparator --- Plastic Biliary Stent (ATLAS Cohort ID: 689).
\item
  \textbf{Outcomes}: Acute cholangitis (Tokyo Guideline 2018, Cohort ID: 697); Moderate acute cholangitis (Cohort ID: 719); Cholangitis broad definition (Cohort ID: 663); All-cause mortality (Cohort ID: 665); Biliary re-intervention.
\item
  \textbf{Design}: New-user active comparator cohort study using 1:1 propensity score matching with large-scale covariates (LASSO logistic regression) and empirical calibration via negative control outcomes.
\item
  \textbf{Timeframe}: Follow-up windows of 30, 90, 180, and 365 days from the index date (first stent insertion).
\end{itemize}

\section{Rationale and Background}\label{rationale-and-background}

Pancreatic cancer is an aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. At the time of diagnosis, the majority of patients present with biliary obstruction, which causes jaundice, pruritus, and cholangitis, significantly impairing quality of life and overall clinical status. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)-guided biliary stent placement is the standard of care for palliating malignant biliary obstruction.

Biliary stents are broadly classified by material into \textbf{Metal Biliary Stents (MS)} and \textbf{Plastic Biliary Stents (PS)}.

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
Characteristic & Metal Stent & Plastic Stent \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
Patency Duration & Long (6--12+ months) & Short (3--6 months) \\
Cost & High & Low \\
Exchangeability & Difficult (self-expanding) & Easy \\
Need for Re-intervention & Low & High \\
\end{longtable}

Current domestic and international guidelines (ESGE, ASGE, KSGE) recommend \textbf{metal stent use} in patients with inoperable malignant biliary obstruction, based on evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showing superior patency and lower re-intervention rates compared to plastic stents {[}\citeproc{ref-Dumonceau2018}{1},\citeproc{ref-Almadi2013}{2}{]}. However, whether these guideline recommendations are equally supported by real-world data remains uncertain.

Existing RCTs may not be representative of real-world clinical populations due to strict eligibility criteria. Real-world comparative evidence on safety (cholangitis) and effectiveness (re-intervention, survival) between the two stent types is limited. A large-scale observational study using the OHDSI Common Data Model (CDM) can validate the applicability of current guidelines in real-world settings.

\section{Study Objectives}\label{study-objectives}

The overall goal of this protocol is conducting comparative safety and effectiveness research to establish evidences for benefits and harms of metal and plastic stents in patients with pancreatic cancer through OHDSI network.

The primary objective is to compare the clinical safety and effectiveness of \textbf{metal biliary stents} versus \textbf{plastic biliary stents} in patients with pancreatic cancer-related biliary obstruction using real-world data.

Secondary objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  To compare the incidence of \textbf{acute cholangitis} between the two stent groups
\item
  To compare the frequency of \textbf{biliary re-intervention} between the two stent groups
\item
  To compare \textbf{all-cause mortality} between the two stent groups
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Methods}\label{research-methods}

\subsection{Study Design}\label{study-design}

This study uses \texttt{CohortMethod} {[}\citeproc{ref-Schuemie2024}{3}{]}.

This is a \textbf{observational study} based on a \textbf{new-user active comparator design} using the OHDSI CohortMethod package for population-level effect estimation (PLE). The new-user design mitigates immortal time bias by requiring all patients to be naive to the study exposure at cohort entry {[}\citeproc{ref-Suissa2008}{4}{]}.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Index Date (Day 0):} Date of first biliary stent insertion (metal or plastic)
\item
  \textbf{Baseline Period:} 365 days prior to the index date (Day −365 to Day 0)
\item
  \textbf{Follow-up Period:} Day 1 through end of observation, death, or end of study period
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Sources}\label{data-sources}

The analyses will be performed across a network of observational healthcare databases. All databases have been transformed into the OMOP Common Data Model, version 5.4. The complete specification for OMOP Common Data Model, version 5.4 is available at: \url{https://github.com/OHDSI/CommonDataModel}

\subsubsection{Biliary Re-intervention}\label{biliary-re-intervention}

\textbf{Definition:} A biliary re-intervention event is defined as any procedure code indicating biliary stent replacement, reinsertion, or repair occurring after Day 7 from the index date (first stent insertion).

\textbf{Identification:} Biliary re-intervention is identified using (1) the same concept sets as the exposure cohorts (metal or plastic biliary stent device exposure codes), and (2) relevant concepts from the Procedure domain, including repeat ERCP, percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) insertion or exchange, and other biliary drainage procedures that do not involve in device exposure. An event is counted as a re-intervention if it occurs more than 7 days after the index date, regardless of the stent type used at re-intervention.

\textbf{Rationale for Day 7 washout:} Events within the first 7 days post-index are excluded to avoid misclassifying planned staged procedures or early technical corrections as re-interventions.

\textbf{Sensitivity Analysis --- Competing Risk of Death}

Given the high mortality rate associated with pancreatic cancer, all-cause death constitutes a competing risk for the acute cholangitis and biliary re-intervention outcomes. Standard Cox/KM approaches that treat death as non-informative censoring may overestimate the cumulative incidence of these outcomes. To address this, the following sensitivity analyses are conducted for the cholangitis and re-intervention outcomes only:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Model:} Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model, with all-cause death specified as a competing risk
\item
  \textbf{Effect Estimate:} Subdistribution Hazard Ratio (sHR) with 95\% Confidence Interval
\item
  \textbf{Visualization:} Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF), accounting for the competing risk of death, presented alongside the cause-specific Cox model results
\end{itemize}

All-cause mortality is not subject to competing risk considerations and is
therefore analyzed using the cause-specific Cox model and Kaplan-Meier
visualization only, as specified above.

\subsubsection{Empirical Calibration}\label{empirical-calibration}

Negative control outcomes are used to assess and correct for residual confounding and systematic bias using the \texttt{EmpiricalCalibration} package {[}\citeproc{ref-Schuemie2018-pnas}{6}{]}.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Negative Controls:} Outcomes with no known biological association with either stent type (see \hyperref[negative-control-concepts]{Negative Control Concepts})
\item
  \textbf{Calibration Procedure:}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Pre-calibration: Assess proportion of negative controls with HR = 1 within 95\% CI
  \item
    Post-calibration: Estimate and correct for systematic bias; derive calibrated effect estimates
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Strengths and Limitations}\label{strengths-limitations}

\subsection{Strengths}\label{strengths}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Real-world data:} Reflects actual clinical populations including patients excluded from RCTs due to strict eligibility criteria
\item
  \textbf{Large-scale propensity score matching:} Hundreds of covariates used for robust confounding control
\item
  \textbf{Empirical calibration:} Quantitative assessment and correction of residual confounding and systematic bias using negative control outcomes
\item
  \textbf{OHDSI standard methodology:} Reproducible and transparent analytical framework
\item
  \textbf{Multiple follow-up windows:} Evaluation at 30, 90, 180, and 365 days covers diverse clinically relevant timepoints
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Observational study limitations:} Absence of randomization means unmeasured confounding cannot be fully excluded
\item
  \textbf{Single database:} Generalizability of results may be limited
\item
  \textbf{Outcome misclassification --- acute cholangitis:} The TG18 definite diagnosis requires imaging evidence of biliary obstruction or dilatation (Category C); however, structured imaging findings are not reliably captured in the OMOP CDM. Consequently, acute cholangitis is operationalized using a \textbf{suspected diagnosis} criterion (Categories A + B only), which may result in outcome misclassification. Cases meeting only laboratory criteria without imaging confirmation may represent other etiologies, potentially inflating the observed incidence of cholangitis.
  4.\textbf{Competing risk of death:} The standard Cox proportional hazards and Kaplan-Meier approach assumes death is a non-informative censoring event. Given the high mortality rate in pancreatic cancer patients, this assumption may be violated, potentially overestimating the cumulative incidence of cholangitis and re-intervention. To mitigate this limitation, a sensitivity analysis using the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model, treating all-cause death as a competing risk, is conducted for these outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Protection of Human Subjects}\label{protection-of-human-subjects}

The study is using only de-identified data. Confidentiality of patient records will be maintained at all times. All study reports will contain aggregate data only and will not identify individual patients or physicians.

\section{Management and Reporting of Adverse Events and Adverse Reactions}\label{management-and-reporting-of-adverse-events-and-adverse-reactions}

This is a non-interventional, retrospective observational study using existing electronic health records. No investigational products are administered and no direct patient interventions are performed. Accordingly, formal adverse event monitoring and reporting procedures applicable to interventional studies are not required. Any incidental findings of clinical significance identified during data analysis will be handled in accordance with applicable institutional policies.
